## Enter the 2023 MacBook Pro 12/18 36GB
First off, this thing is beautiful. It's more a work of art than a computer. Everything is as it should be, and this is by design. When Apple transitioned away from Intel to their "M" series chips, they integrated everything onto a single die. This means that the CPU, GPU, Neural Engine all share a single pool of memory, which keeps latency down, and makes the whole system need less memory, as it doesn't have to store data in multiple places. This thing is blazing fast.

Anyone can make a chip. With enough money, resources, and time, you can design great hardware. The magic is in the way that the software and hardware are integrated so closely. Since the software knows the exact geometry of the hardware, it can work much more efficiently and closer to the hardware. They aren't building an operating system for any "discreet", off the shelf component. They know exactly what hardware this software will be running on top of, and have minmaxed the shit out of the OS.

My MacBook Pro in specific has the following specs:
* 16" model (having seen the 14" model, I'm so glad I opted for the bigger one)
* 4.05 GHz Apple M3 Pro processor with twelve cores (6 performance cores and 6 efficiency cores)
* 18-core GPU
* 16-core Neural Engine
* 36 GB of unified onboard RAM
* 512 GB SSD (external SSDs will be needed)

## Apple fanboy
I wouldn't sing the praises of this thing if it did not deserve it. It is well integrated, quick, and just overall a good experience. I'm not going to talk about the "Ecosystem". I think it's cool that everything works together, but a lot of it I don't even use. I have an Apple TV that works well. Will I answer calls on it? No. I have an iPhone. It's nice and works well.

My favorite part about this "Ecosystem" is AirDrop. I wish that it was an open standard.
## But what about X?
* Windows software: Wine, Whiskey, Compatibility Layers
* Gaming: I don't game as much these days. Those that I do want to play (mostly) don't have kernel level anti cheats or have support for mac. (Thank GOD Rust has a Mac port)
* Windows as a whole: Microsoft has made it clear that they don't care about the user's privacy (see windows recall), choices, or hardware they have bought (your old pc *can* run Windows 11, we just don't want it to???)

## Growing pains
There are certain things (honestly, few and far between) that irk me about MacOS. The beauty is that most of them can be changed. I'm still tweaking and learning how to get some windows functionality into MacOS (especially in finder), but by & large I really enjoy the stock MacOS experience. I am used to a certain level of "Power User"-ness that MacOS does not have by default. To be fair, Windows didn't really either. I'm still figuring that part out.

## Overall
I really do like this new machine. I love that it is snappy. I have a work laptop with an Intel Core Ultra 9 285HX and 128GB of DDR5. It is powerful in theory, but also does not feel as snappy as this machine (that by numbers alone is not nearly as powerful). I'm sold on Apple Laptops, and I'm (pretty much) sold on MacOS.

Somewhere down the line, I might buy a nice gaming laptop just for windows tasks (and gaming!), but right now this suits me just fine, and feels great.
